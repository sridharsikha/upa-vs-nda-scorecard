UPA vs NDA — Total & Potential

India, 2004–2014 vs 2014–2026 · a framework for argument, not a scoreboard

Context & caveats

Read this first. It sets out what the model already prices in, what it doesn't, and where the numbers are soft.

The two-sided model

TOTAL = GDP and hard counts. Real data. What the economy actually did on their watch. Not a judgment call.

POTENTIAL = a 1–10 judgment on four components. Two positives (what you built, what you rescued), two negatives (what you broke, what you wasted). Every number is hindsight opinion and is yours to move.

Cheap oil — already priced in

Crude fell from ~$110 to ~$30 across 2014–16, and India imports roughly 85% of its oil: a once-in-a-generation fiscal gift. That is exactly what Modi's squander 7 encodes — the windfall was largely absorbed into excise rather than passed to consumers or converted into a lasting asset. Without that single score he is net-positive. It is the number carrying the whole argument.

Majority vs coalition — not yet priced in

This cuts against NDA and is currently missing. UPA-I governed on Left outside support and UPA-II on a full coalition. NDA had a BJP single-party majority in 2014 (282 seats) and 2019 (303) — and by the same squander logic, a majority is a good hand. If you weight it, either Modi's squander rises or a fifth "mandate converted" component gets added.

One constraint on doing so: NDA-III (2024, BJP 240) is a coalition again, dependent on TDP and JD(U). Any mandate adjustment should apply to 2014–24, not the full run.

Crisis symmetry

UPA absorbed the 2008 global financial crisis and the 2013 taper tantrum. NDA absorbed COVID. Neither side gets to claim a clean run, and the crisis-converted component is where that belongs.

Four things to fix before citing this publicly

1. Every potential score is hindsight judgment taking a position in a live political fight. A BJP partisan and a Congress partisan would each call half of it rigged. "A framework for argument" is the only honest framing.

2. Several Total-column multiples flatter whoever they favour. Solar and semiconductors start from near-zero bases. Figures like 520+ GW capacity, 110 GW solar and 33× defence exports looked rounded-up or optimistic in the source table — verify each before citing.

3. The highways row has a definitional seam. National highway length goes 79,116 km (2013) → 91,287 km (2014) in a single year. That ~12,000 km jump is state highways being reclassified as NH, not road built. The NDA-decade addition is still large, but the start-point should be checked before the "4× the pace" claim goes public.

4. GDP rates straddle a methodology change. Growth figures spanning 2004–2026 cross the 2015 base-year revision (2004-05 → 2011-12 series). The 7.7% and 6.0% averages are not measured on an identical basis end to end.

The verdict this table supports

Most rows are compounding on an existing trajectory, not a regime break. Two things survive the test: national highways (a genuine acceleration in pace) and the three inclusion programmes — Jan Dhan, Swachh Bharat, Ujjwala — which are step-functions with no UPA analogue. Jan Dhan is the most consequential, because financial inclusion is the substrate the entire digital-payments stack sits on.

Why the two columns disagree

The sharpest thing this exercise produces is that GDP and potential rarely agree. Manmohan posts the highest growth on the board and scores near zero on potential. Rao posts a middling 5.1% and scores the highest potential by a wide margin — the planter the economy didn't reward in his own years. Growth is what the data rewarded; potential is what judgment rewards.

Reading the board

Only four PMs come out net-positive once squander and damage are counted, and Nehru barely — his enormous plant is nearly cancelled by his enormous damage. The pattern that separates the top from the bottom is not growth: it's what hand they were dealt and what they did with it. Rao and Shastri were handed crises and converted them. Rajiv and Modi were handed gifts — a record mandate, a collapsed oil price — and the model says they banked less of it.

Two arithmetic notes

Indira does not reconcile. Her net was stated as +2.5, but her components (6.5 + 7 − 9 − 4) sum to +0.5. She is plotted at +0.5. Crisis 7 versus damage 9 is the most load-bearing pair in the model — it is what decides whether she sits above or below the line at all.

Vajpayee was never restated. His +3 comes from the earlier three-component version; no crisis-converted score was ever assigned, so his bar is not directly comparable to the others. Crisis scores marked derived elsewhere were back-calculated from the difference between the three- and four-component nets.
